Can sleep apnea occur together with REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD)?

Internal Medicine

Yes, sleep apnea and REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD) can occur together. Research shows many people with RBD also experience obstructive sleep apnea, a condition that disrupts breathing during sleep. When these disorders overlap, they can worsen sleep quality, increase daytime fatigue, and complicate diagnosis. Symptoms may include acting out dreams, loud snoring, gasping for air, or waking unrefreshed. Because both conditions share overlapping signs and can point to underlying neurological or cardiovascular concerns, identifying them early matters. Sleep apnea and REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD) can happen at the same time. Sleep apnea is when a person stops breathing for short periods while sleeping. RBD is when people act out their dreams because their muscles don't relax during REM sleep.

Connection Between RBD and Sleep Apnea:

People with RBD often have sleep apnea too. This means they might stop breathing while they are acting out their dreams.

Research Findings:

One study found that many people with RBD also have sleep apnea. This suggests a link between the two conditions.

Another study mentioned a 'benefit' where the lack of muscle relaxation in RBD might actually help some people breathe better during REM sleep.
