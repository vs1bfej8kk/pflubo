How to Identify if Ashwagandha Is Causing an Autoimmune Flare

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is a popular adaptogenic herb known for its stress-relieving and immune-modulating properties. While many people with autoimmune conditions use ashwagandha to help restore balance, a small subset may experience worsened symptoms—or autoimmune flares—after starting it. Understanding whether ashwagandha is behind a flare can help you manage your health safely.

Understanding Autoimmune Flares

An autoimmune flare occurs when the body's immune system becomes overactive, attacking its own tissues. Common autoimmune diseases include:

Rheumatoid arthritis

Lupus (systemic lupus erythematosus)

Multiple sclerosis

Inflammatory bowel disease

During a flare, you might notice new or intensified symptoms such as joint pain, skin rashes, fatigue, or digestive upset. Because ashwagandha influences immune function, it's important to recognize warning signs that it may be contributing to a flare.

Why Ashwagandha Could Trigger Autoimmune Flares

Ashwagandha is generally considered safe, but its immune-stimulating effects can sometimes backfire in sensitive individuals:

ImmunomodulationResearch (PubMed, NIH Office of Dietary Supplements) shows ashwagandha can enhance certain immune responses—potentially over-activating immune cells in autoimmune disease.

Hormonal effectsAshwagandha may influence cortisol and thyroid hormones. Fluctuations in these hormones can indirectly affect immune regulation.

Individual sensitivityGenetic factors, existing medications, and the underlying type of autoimmune disease all play a role in how you react.

Common Signs That Ashwagandha May Be Causing an Autoimmune Flare

Increased stiffness in the morning

New swelling or redness around joints

Rashes, hives, or psoriasis patches

Itching or burning sensations

Increased bloating, cramping, or diarrhea

When to Seek Urgent Medical Attention

Some symptoms may indicate a serious flare or complication. Contact your doctor or seek emergency care if you experience:

Sudden, severe joint swelling with unbearable pain

High fever (above 101.5°F/38.6°C) unresponsive to acetaminophen

Difficulty breathing, chest pain, or rapid heartbeat

Neurological changes (vision loss, severe headache, weakness)

Balancing Benefits and Risks

Ashwagandha can offer benefits—stress relief, improved sleep, enhanced resilience—but its immune-boosting action isn't right for everyone. Use these guidelines:

Start with a low dose (200–300 mg standardized extract)

Increase gradually, monitoring symptoms

Pair with calming lifestyle habits (meditation, balanced nutrition)

If flares persist despite adjustments, it may be best to switch to a different adaptogen (e.g., holy basil) or discontinue herbal support altogether.

Talking to Your Doctor

Always involve your healthcare provider in decisions about supplements—especially if you have an autoimmune condition. A doctor can:

Review your complete medical history

Order appropriate lab tests

Adjust prescription medications safely

Recommend evidence-based alternatives

Never ignore persistent or life-threatening symptoms. Your health and safety come first.
