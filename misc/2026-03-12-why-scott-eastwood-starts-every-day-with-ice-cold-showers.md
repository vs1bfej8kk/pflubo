# Why Scott Eastwood Starts Every Day With Ice Cold Showers

Scott Eastwood can only talk for 20 minutes before he has to hop on a plane to Los Angeles, but such is life for a guy promoting a blockbuster film like Pacific Rim: Uprising while also serving as the spokesperson for Davidoff's Cool Water fragrance. When MensHealth.com sat down with the actor at the Gramercy Park Hotel, he was in the midst of a global publicity tour that took him from Australia to China to London, and, now, to New York. Add to the jet lag roughly a few hundred conversations about fighting giant monsters in director Stephen S. DeKnight's massive CGI spectacle, and Eastwood should be exhausted.

But the thing about Scott Eastwood is that he doesn't really get tired. He's laid back, sure. Not only is his ocean-breeze SoCal drawl a far cry from his father Clint's intense cowboy growl, the younger Eastwood juggles yoga and surfing almost as much as modeling and high-budget action flicks. It's what drew him to Davidoff—which partners with National Geographic's Pristine Seas project to help preserve the Earth's oceans—in the first place.

"Davidoff is set apart because it has an incredible social cause behind it," he tells us. "It all marries together so perfectly. It’s about the ocean. For me, that’s number one. I’m a huge ocean guy. I grew up by the sea, doing every ocean activity from fishing to diving. I’ve been extremely interested in marine conservation ever since I was a little kid."

But just because he's chill, doesn't mean he's weary. Despite the grueling travel schedule, Eastwood exudes that special kind of infectious energy unique to people who spend life in front of a camera.

The key, he tells us, is to use the time you're not kicking bad guys' asses onscreen to kick your own ass in the gym. Also: dousing yourself in cold water before the sun comes up.

"Sure, when you're planning your life at the 30,000-foot level there's a great movie career and all that, yada yada yada," he says. "But on a daily basis you can only control what you can control. You can control your body and your health, how you schedule your life, your habits." He runs a hand through a newly grown beard, grinning. "Whatever effort you put forth, it all starts with your health."

For Eastwood, health starts with establishing forward momentum. Once he's up — Eastwood starts his day at 5 a.m. — he's focused on getting his heart pumping. He recommends finding a source of cold water and stepping in. "That’ll wake you up better than any cup of coffee," he says. (If the 5 a.m. ice bath or cold shower sounds a little too intense, here are some other tips.)

"It’s about knowing how good you feel afterward," he continues. "If you can have that at the forefront of your mind it’s pretty easy to push past anything. I know I’m going to feel an endorphin high after I get in this gym, my metabolism is going to be working on overtime, it’ll be humming. Everything just feels better when you’re red-lining your metabolism."

Which isn't a terrible metaphor for Eastwood's career as a whole. His early acting experience — his cold shower, if you will — was a brief appearance in his father's 2008 film Gran Torino. He's been red-lining his acting metabolism ever since, jumping from Taylor Swift music videos, to DC Comics films, to installments in the Fast and the Furious franchise. Now, he's starring in what could be one of 2018's biggest blockbusters.

Eastwood chalks his hustle up to an ability to plan his next steps one-by-one, a career-elevating skill he carried over from his life-long dedication to fitness.

"Feeling mentally strong is the key to taking the next step in the day," he says. "OK, done with the gym. It’s 6 a.m. Boom, what am I doing next? Laying those next steps out in front of you is easier if you’re already firing on all cylinders."

Bring on the ice bath.
