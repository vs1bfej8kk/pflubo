Doctor's Note: Which Thyroid Labs Must Black Women Check Before Starting Mounjaro?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Before starting Mounjaro, Black women should ask their provider for a full thyroid panel: TSH, free T4, free T3, thyroid peroxidase antibodies, thyroglobulin antibodies, and optionally reverse T3. This testing helps rule out hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, and autoimmune thyroid conditions like Hashimoto's or Graves' disease—all of which can slow weight loss on Mounjaro or affect treatment safety.

Explanation

When considering Mounjaro (tirzepatide) for weight management or type 2 diabetes, it's vital to ensure your thyroid is functioning properly. Thyroid hormones influence metabolism, energy levels, and body weight. For Black women—who may face unique risk factors for thyroid dysfunction—getting the right thyroid lab tests before starting Mounjaro can help you and your healthcare team tailor treatment safely and effectively.

Why Thyroid Lab Tests Matter with Mounjaro

Mounjaro can lead to significant weight loss. Untreated thyroid disease (especially hypothyroidism) can blunt weight-loss efforts or mask underlying issues.

Key Thyroid Lab Tests to Order

Before you start Mounjaro, ask your doctor to include these thyroid lab tests:

Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH)

The primary screening test for thyroid function.

High TSH suggests underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism); low TSH suggests overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism).

Free Thyroxine (Free T4)

Measures the unbound T4 hormone available to tissues.

Helps distinguish between subclinical and overt hypothyroidism when TSH is elevated.

Free Triiodothyronine (Free T3)

Active hormone form affecting metabolism.

Useful if you have persistent symptoms with normal TSH/free T4 or if you're on T4 therapy.

Thyroid Peroxidase Antibodies (TPO-Ab)

Detects autoimmune thyroiditis (Hashimoto's disease).

Positive TPO-Ab can predict progression to overt hypothyroidism.

Thyroglobulin Antibodies (Tg-Ab)

Another marker for autoimmune thyroid disease.

Often checked alongside TPO-Ab for a complete autoimmune profile.

Reverse T3 (rT3)

Optional test; measures inactive form of T3.

Can help in complex cases where conversion of T4 to T3 is impaired (e.g., chronic stress, poor nutrition).

Why These Tests Matter for Black Women

Vitamin D DeficiencyBlack women have higher rates of vitamin D deficiency, which can influence thyroid autoimmunity. Low vitamin D is linked to higher TPO-Ab levels.

Autoimmune Thyroid DiseaseWhile Hashimoto's tends to be more common in white women, autoimmune markers still occur and contribute to fatigue and weight gain.

Health DisparitiesDelays in diagnosis and treatment of thyroid disorders are more common in Black communities. Proactive testing helps bridge this gap.

How to Prepare for Thyroid Lab Testing

Timing:Most thyroid labs can be drawn any time of day, but aim for morning if you're on thyroid hormone replacement.

Medications & Supplements:Let your provider know about biotin, iron or calcium supplements—these can interfere with thyroid assays.

Fasting:Fasting isn't strictly required for thyroid tests, but follow your lab's instructions.

Interpreting Common Results

TSH

High → hypothyroid; Low → hyperthyroid

Free T4

Low → hypothyroid; High → hyperthyroid

Free T3

Low → poor conversion; High → excess hormone

TPO-Ab

< 35 IU/mL

Positive → autoimmune thyroiditis

Tg-Ab

< 20 IU/mL

Positive → autoimmune involvement

rT3

10–24 ng/dL

High → stress-related conversion issues

Ranges can vary by lab. Always review results with your doctor.

Common Questions About Mounjaro and Thyroid Lab Tests

"Can I start Mounjaro if my thyroid labs are slightly off?"Mild, subclinical thyroid changes may not delay Mounjaro but do require monitoring.

"Could Mounjaro affect my thyroid function?"There's no direct evidence that tirzepatide worsens thyroid function, but significant weight changes can unmask underlying thyroid disease.

"How often should my thyroid labs be checked?"Typically every 6–12 weeks until stable, then every 6–12 months unless symptoms change.
