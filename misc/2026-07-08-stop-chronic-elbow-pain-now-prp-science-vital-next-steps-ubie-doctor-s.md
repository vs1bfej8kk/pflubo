Why Is Your Elbow Pain Chronic? The Science of PRP and Medical Next Steps

Chronic elbow pain—defined as pain lasting more than three months—can disrupt daily activities, work, and sports. Understanding why your elbow pain lingers is the first step toward relief. In recent years, PRP (platelet-rich plasma) has emerged as a promising option. Below, we explore common causes of chronic elbow pain, the science behind PRP for chronic elbow pain, and practical next steps to help you get back to the activities you love.

What Causes Chronic Elbow Pain?

Elbow pain that doesn't resolve on its own often stems from one or more of the following:

Tendinopathy (Tennis/Golfer's Elbow)• Lateral epicondylitis ("tennis elbow") – overuse of forearm extensors• Medial epicondylitis ("golfer's elbow") – overuse of wrist flexors

Arthritis• Osteoarthritis – wear and tear of joint cartilage• Rheumatoid arthritis – autoimmune inflammation

Bursitis• Olecranon bursitis – fluid-filled sac at the tip of the elbow becomes inflamed

Nerve Compression• Cubital tunnel syndrome – ulnar nerve irritation at the elbow

Post-Traumatic Changes• Fracture malunion or scar tissue from a past injury

Instability• Repeated sprains or ligament injuries leading to looseness in the joint

When tendon fibers or joint structures fail to heal properly, the body may trigger ongoing inflammation, microtears, and scar tissue formation. This creates a cycle of pain and stiffness that resists simple rest or anti-inflammatory medications.

What Is PRP and How Does It Work?

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) uses your own blood's healing power. Here's a step-by-step look:

Blood DrawA small amount of your blood is collected, similar to a routine lab test.

CentrifugationThe sample is spun in a centrifuge to concentrate platelets and growth factors.

InjectionThe platelet-rich layer is injected directly into the injured tendon or joint under ultrasound guidance.

Clinical studies on PRP for tendon injuries at the elbow show:

Tennis Elbow (Lateral Epicondylitis)• Multiple randomized trials demonstrate greater pain relief and improved function at 6–12 months compared with steroid injections.• PRP may reduce recurrence rates.

Accurate Diagnosis

Who Is a Good Candidate for PRP?

PRP is most effective when:

You have chronic tendinopathy (tennis or golfer's elbow) unresponsive to 3–6 months of conservative care.

Imaging shows partial tendon tears or degeneration without complete rupture.

You are in generally good health and can participate in post-injection rehab.

Discuss any blood disorders, anticoagulant use, or active infections with your doctor, as these may affect your suitability for PRP.

Potential Risks and Downsides

PRP is generally safe, but be aware of:

Temporary increase in pain or stiffness at the injection site

Infection risk (very low with proper sterile technique)

Cost and insurance coverage variability

Uncertain results—some patients may see minimal benefit

Your doctor can help you weigh these factors against the potential for improved healing and reduced reliance on repeated steroid injections.

When to Seek Urgent Medical Attention

While most chronic elbow pain isn't life-threatening, you should seek prompt care if you experience:

Sudden, severe pain after an injury

Visible deformity or inability to straighten the elbow

Signs of infection: redness, warmth, fever

Remember: Speak to a Doctor

Chronic elbow pain can interfere with work, hobbies, and quality of life.
