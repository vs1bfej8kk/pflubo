Stage 4 prostate cancer survival varies significantly, but many people live several years after diagnosis. According to recent data, the 5-year relative survival rate for metastatic (stage 4) prostate cancer is approximately 34%, though outcomes depend on several key factors:

Where the cancer has spread (bones, lymph nodes, or distant organs)

Overall health and age at diagnosis

Response to treatment (hormone therapy, chemotherapy, radiation, or newer targeted therapies)

Gleason score and PSA levels

Whether the cancer is hormone-sensitive or castration-resistant

Advances in treatment—including immunotherapy, precision medicine, and combination therapies—have improved life expectancy in recent years. Some patients live 5 to 10+ years with proper management, especially when the cancer responds well to hormone therapy.

Stage 4 prostate cancer means the cancer has spread to other parts of the body. How long someone can live with this stage depends on many things, like their overall health and how well they respond to treatment.

Five-Year Survival: According to a 2023 study, some patients with late-stage prostate cancer can live for five years or more.

Ten-Year Survival: Another study from 2003 found that a small number of patients with metastatic prostate cancer lived for ten years.
