# ​The Complete History of the Fanny Pack

A soccer mom at Disney, a tourist in socks and flipflops, a grandfather using a metal detector at a crowded beach—this is the demographic of people still wearing fanny packs.

A little language lesson: The word fanny has a different (and less PG-13) meaning in Britain, where they call the accessory a “bum bag.” A rose is still a rose by any other name though. The fanny pack was once a huge global phenomenon until it became so popular, it reached the point of mockery.

We would hate to call the fanny pack the new comeback kid, but the needle seems to tip that way. Kanye West, Ovadia & Sons, Telfar, and multiple other menswear brands all showed some form of fanny packs during New York Fashion Week.

We’re seeking answers. First question: Dear lord, why? Second question: How did the humble hip-bag get its start?

~3,300 B.C.: The Ice-Cold Look

In 1991, tourists in the Alps stumbled upon the mummified body of fashion influencer Ötzi Iceman. The ancient dead man had a pouch sewn to his belt containing only the necessities: a scraper, drill, flint flake, bone awl, and a piece of dried fungus (not that we know what a bone awl is).

3000 B.C.: Dress Like An Egyptian

We don’t believe they had a symbol for the term “fanny pack,” but hieroglyphs do show ancient Egyptians sporting what looks like an ancient Jansport cop. The bible also refers to Judas Iscariot as a “purse carrier,” meaning not a handbag but a small bag hung from the girdle at the back—making him know for both betraying Jesus and rocking a hip-bag.

16th to 20th Century: Outfitted For Battles Of Yesteryear

In modern times, people with concealed carry permits love to hide their handguns in fanny packs (so maybe you should keep your jokes to yourself). The soldiers of yore also loved a good leather bag on their sword belts, or more recently, ammunition belts.

White settlers during the 17th century also recorded that Native Americans fastened pouches to a belt “bound about their middles” for more peaceful purposes.

1960s and '70s: Made For The Mountains

Oxford dictionary’s earliest reference to fanny packs comes from a linguistics journal in 1963, which references them as slang for the belt that ski patrol used to carry their first aid kit.

Skiers, hikers, and other outdoorsy adventurers helped popularize fanny packs for the mass market in garish neons and synthetic fabrics. REI still sells “waist packs” for anyone feeling nostalgic.

1980s: The Golden Age Of The Fanny Pack

The packs transitioned from hiking wear to street apparel this decade as part of larger trend toward athleisure, Jennifer Grayer Moore writes in her book on fashion fads. Trade publication Adweek even declared the fanny pack the hottest product of the year in 1988.

Companies like Molson beer and NBA teams gave them out to fans as freebies to fans in nylon and neoprene fabrics. For leather packs, the LA Times even offered some sweet styling suggestions in 1989, telling readers they “look chic in the same way leather jackets do.”

Ever the trendsetter, Terminator X of Public Enemy was photographed in the Village Voice wearing both a beeper and a leather fanny pack in 1989 (and still looking quite reserved next to a ticking Flavor Flav).

1990s: America Reaches Pack Overload

The bag still ruled heading into the next decade. A fanny pack appeared in a Vogue editorial in September 1990. Dwayne “The Rock” Johnson graced America with an elegant photo of him in a fanny pack, which he later told Jimmy Kimmel was filled with Poptarts and condoms. Sounds reasonable.

“Any time you have a leather fanny pack, one must insert the thumb behind the pack,” the Rock advised Kimmel. “It’s a chick magnet.”

By the middle of the decade though, Grayer Moore says its overwhelming popularity with “middle class, middle aged, convenience-oriented consumers” caused fashionistas to renounce the accessory.

Things went quiet on the pack front until high-fashion brands began to rework it at the end of the decade. They might have called it a “belted satchel,” but you can’t hide the true colors of a fanny pack—even if it’s from Louis Vuitton.

2,000s to Early 2010s: A Lighter Load

Hipsters were the first to strap back on fanny packs as an ironic fashion statement. Mainstream fashion houses, such as Chanel and Alexander Wang, again tried to make “belt bags” happen later in the new millennium, but fanny packs found a more committed home with female festival goers and ravers.

Two early male trendsetters—Leonardo Dicaprio and Jared Leto—were both spotted frolicking on the beach in fanny packs. Leto really invested in the #hippacklife—as he called it—and briefly sold fanny packs in two different colorways on his band's website. Today, Dicaprio continues to wear fanny packs so often that New York Magazine speculated he uses them to carry around his Oscar.

2015 and Onward: Fanny Packs Snap Back

The website fannypackmaniac.com has kindly ranked the top fanny releases from 2015 and 2016 for us. You should probably use their lists to pick yours now, as the fall 2017 runway shows heralded a new dawn of the fanny pack. Designers slung them at all sorts of angles over denim, sweatpants, denim/sweatpants hybrids—you name it.

The NYPD even tweeted summer styling advice on fanny packs for you in 2015, instructing wearers to position it in the front where it is “hideous but protected.” Groundbreaking.
