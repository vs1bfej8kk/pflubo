# How To Adjust Your Squat Stance Based on Your Anatomy

Dr. Aaron Horschig, DPT, of Squat University is always looking to help lifters fix their form when it comes to injury prevention, especially when it comes to squats. He's shown us how to avoid lower back pain while lifting and the best ankle mobility exercises for better squats. Now, he's sharing his knowledge about how your personal anatomy determines how your form should change while squatting.

Should you squat with toes 100 percent straight forward, or turned out to the side? Horschig demonstrates how to screen your body and determine your best squat stance based on your hip anatomy.

"In textbook anatomy [the way most people's bodies work], your femur connects to your pelvis at a slight angle forward. However, some people don't have that textbook anatomy alignment of the femur," he says. "Their hips may be more angled forward, which is called anteversion, or their hips may be naturally more flat in their connection, which is called retroversion."

And depending on which anatomy variation you have, it can have a dramatic effect on what may feel most natural for you when it comes to squatting, which can affect how well you move and how much you can lift.

To determine your own anatomy, there are two a simple screening tests you can do at home.

Seated External and Internal Rotation Test

To do this, all you have to do is sit on a table and test your external and internal rotations.

"Someone with anteverson will have internal rotation excessive in one of the two seated positions," says Dr. Horschig. He demonstrates this on a model, who displays excessive rotation internally, and very little external rotation.

From there, he has her flip onto her stomach for another test.
