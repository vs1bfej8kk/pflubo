Reviewed by: Dr. Hal and Gameday's Medical Advisory Board

Clomiphene citrate, commonly known as Clomid, is a medication that has been used for decades to treat infertility in women. But in recent years, it's gained attention for its potential to improve male fertility by increasing testosterone levels and sperm production.

What is Clomiphene Citrate?

Clomiphene citrate is an oral medication that belongs to a class of drugs called selective estrogen receptor modulators (SERMs). It works by blocking estrogen receptors in the hypothalamus, a region of the brain that regulates hormone production. By blocking estrogen's negative feedback loop, Clomid stimulates the pituitary gland to release more follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH), which in turn promotes testosterone production in the testes.

Clomid is typically prescribed in 50mg tablets, with dosages ranging from 25mg to 100mg per day, depending on the individual's needs and response to treatment. The medication is usually taken for a period of 3-6 months, during which time the patient's hormone levels and semen parameters are closely monitored.
