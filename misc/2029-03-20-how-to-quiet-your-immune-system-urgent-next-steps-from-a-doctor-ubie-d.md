How to Quiet the Immune System: Important Next Steps from a Doctor

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Calming an overactive immune system in autoimmune conditions requires a multi-pronged approach: a targeted elimination diet, stress management, gentle movement, key supplements, and consistent medical monitoring. Together, these strategies help reduce chronic inflammation and restore immune balance based on your unique triggers.

Key steps include:

Elimination diet: Remove common inflammatory triggers (gluten, dairy, sugar, processed foods) for 4–6 weeks, then reintroduce systematically.

Stress management: Practice daily meditation, deep breathing, or yoga to lower cortisol.

Gentle movement: Walking, swimming, or tai chi support circulation without overtaxing the body.

Supplements: Consider vitamin D, omega-3s, curcumin, and probiotics (with provider guidance).

Medical monitoring: Regular labs and prescription treatments when needed.

5. Medical Treatments and Monitoring

For moderate to severe autoimmune disease, diet and lifestyle may need to be paired with medical therapies.

Non-steroidal anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs) for pain relief.

Disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) or biologics to specifically target immune pathways.

Track inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR) and organ-specific antibodies.

Monitor vitamin and mineral levels (D, B12, iron).

Ultrasound or MRI may be used for joint-focused diseases to assess progression.

Working closely with a rheumatologist, endocrinologist, or other specialist ensures your plan remains effective and safe.
