Vitamin D and Erectile Dysfunction: What the Research Says

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Can low vitamin D cause erectile dysfunction? Yes — research links low vitamin D to higher rates of erectile dysfunction (ED), and a randomized trial showed that correcting deficiency improved erectile function in some men. Vitamin D influences ED through vascular, inflammatory, and hormonal pathways.

Before starting supplements, get your 25(OH)D level tested and speak with a clinician about safe dosing and monitoring. ED can also be an early warning sign of cardiovascular or metabolic disease, so it's important not to ignore it.

Explanation

Erectile dysfunction (ED) affects millions of men worldwide, impacting confidence and quality of life. In recent years, researchers have explored the role of vitamin D—an essential nutrient involved in bone health, immune function and cardiovascular health—in sexual function. Below, we break down key studies and what they mean for using vitamin D for erectile dysfunction.

Understanding Erectile Dysfunction and Vitamin D

Erectile dysfunction occurs when a man has difficulty achieving or maintaining an erection sufficient for sexual activity. Common causes include:

Poor blood flow due to vascular disease

Hormonal imbalances (e.g., low testosterone)

Nerve damage

Psychological factors (stress, anxiety, depression)

Vitamin D, measured as serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D], is best known for supporting bone strength. However, it also:

Helps regulate blood pressure and blood-vessel health

Modulates inflammation

Influences smooth muscle function, including in penile tissue

These mechanisms have prompted investigation into whether optimizing vitamin D status could help men with ED.

Key Study 1: Cross-Sectional Link Between Vitamin D Levels and ED

Li et al. (2019) conducted a cross-sectional analysis of over 3,000 men to examine the relationship between serum 25(OH)D levels and ED risk. Main findings included:

Men with vitamin D deficiency (<20 ng/mL) had a significantly higher rate of ED than men with sufficient levels (>30 ng/mL).

After adjusting for age, body mass index (BMI), diabetes, hypertension and other factors, low vitamin D remained independently associated with a greater risk of ED.

Each 10 ng/mL increase in 25(OH)D corresponded to roughly a 20 percent lower odds of ED.

While cross-sectional studies can't prove cause and effect, these data suggest a consistent link between low vitamin D and poorer erectile function.

Key Study 2: Randomized Trial of Vitamin D Supplementation

Aykac et al. (2018) performed a randomized, placebo-controlled trial in men diagnosed with both hypovitaminosis D (25(OH)D <30 ng/mL) and mild to moderate ED. Participants received either:

Vitamin D3 50,000 IU weekly for 12 weeks

Placebo

Results after 12 weeks:

The supplement group experienced a significant improvement in the International Index of Erectile Function (IIEF-5) score compared to placebo.

Serum 25(OH)D levels rose by an average of 15 ng/mL in the treatment group.

No serious adverse events were reported; some men reported mild gastrointestinal discomfort.

This trial provides preliminary evidence that correcting vitamin D deficiency can lead to measurable improvements in erectile function.

Key Study 3: Systematic Review of Vitamin D in Aging Men

Annweiler et al. (2019) reviewed all available clinical trials assessing vitamin D-related treatments for ED in older men. Their systematic review noted:

A small number of high-quality randomized trials exist.

Most studies reported favorable effects of vitamin D supplementation on erectile function, particularly in men who were deficient at baseline.

Benefits appeared more pronounced when vitamin D status was severely low.

Research gaps remain regarding optimal dosing, duration and long-term outcomes.

The review concluded that while promising, more large-scale trials are needed to confirm benefits and refine treatment protocols.

How Vitamin D Might Improve Erectile Function

Several biological mechanisms may explain why vitamin D status affects erections:

Vascular health: Vitamin D promotes nitric oxide production in blood vessels, aiding relaxation and improved blood flow to the penis.

Anti-inflammatory effects: Reducing chronic inflammation may protect endothelial (blood-vessel lining) function.

Hormonal balance: Adequate vitamin D supports testosterone synthesis, which is crucial for libido and erection quality.

Smooth muscle function: Vitamin D receptors in penile tissue influence muscle tone and responsiveness.

By supporting these pathways, vitamin D could help address some underlying contributors to ED.

Practical Recommendations

If you're considering vitamin D for erectile dysfunction, keep the following in mind:

Check your vitamin D status. A simple blood test measures serum 25(OH)D. Optimal levels generally range from 30–50 ng/mL.

If you're deficient, discuss supplementation with your doctor. Typical doses range from 1,000 to 4,000 IU daily, though higher therapeutic doses may be used short-term under medical supervision.

Regular exercise (especially aerobic activity)

Balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables and healthy fats

Weight management if overweight

Limiting smoking and excessive alcohol

Who Might Benefit Most?

Men over age 50, when vitamin D levels naturally decline.

Individuals with limited sun exposure or darker skin tone (higher risk of deficiency).

Men with other cardiovascular risk factors (hypertension, diabetes, high cholesterol).

Those with documented low baseline 25(OH)D levels and mild to moderate ED.

Safety and Monitoring

Vitamin D is generally safe at recommended doses, but excessive intake can cause:

Hypercalcemia (high blood calcium)

Kidney stones or impaired kidney function

Nausea, vomiting or weakness

To stay safe:

Follow your doctor's dosing guidance.

Avoid self-prescribing very high doses for long periods.

Have your blood levels rechecked after 3–6 months of supplementation.

Report any unusual symptoms (e.g., persistent stomach upset, excessive thirst).
