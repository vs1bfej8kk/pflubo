Sleep apnea can sometimes be treated without a CPAP machine. Alternatives include weight loss, positional therapy, oral appliances that reposition the jaw, and surgical options to remove airway obstructions. The best approach depends on the severity of your condition, its underlying cause, and your overall health.

Because sleep apnea symptoms often overlap with other conditions—like fatigue, snoring, or morning headaches—it's important to understand what may actually be driving your symptoms before choosing a treatment path. A quick, free symptom check can help you identify possible causes in minutes and guide you toward the right next steps, whether that's exploring non-CPAP options or speaking with a specialist.

Sleep apnea is a condition where breathing stops and starts during sleep. While using a CPAP machine is common, there are other ways to help manage sleep apnea.

Non-CPAP Options:

Weight Loss: Losing weight can reduce the severity of sleep apnea for some people.

Oral Devices: Special mouthpieces can help keep the airway open.

Surgery: In some cases, surgery can remove or shrink tissue in the throat or reposition the jaw.

Positional Therapy: Changing sleeping positions, like sleeping on your side, might help.

These methods may not work for everyone, and their effectiveness can vary. If you're experiencing symptoms like snoring, daytime fatigue, or interrupted breathing during sleep, you can check your symptoms with a free AI-powered assessment to better understand your condition. It's important to talk with a healthcare provider to find the best approach for managing sleep apnea. Each person's situation is different, so what works for one person might not work for another.
