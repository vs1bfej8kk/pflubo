# Chris Evans Turned Down Captain America TWICE

Chris Evans turned down the role of Steve Rogers twice before finally being convinced to take the part, a Hollywood Reporter profile of the actor revealed this week. Apparently it was “an encouraging call” from Iron Man himself, Robert Downey Jr., that finally convinced him to say yes.

“Getting the offer felt to me like the epitome of temptation,” Evans said. “The ultimate job offer, on the biggest scale. I’m supposed to say no to this thing. It felt like the right thing to do.” Marvel kept knocking on his door though, taking him on a studio tour and notching down the nine-movie commitment to six, but it was only when Robert Downey Jr. contacted him that Evans seriously reconsidered.

Evans eventually decided to suit up, and immediately sought out a therapist to help him through this major life decision (he revealed in the interview that he still loves therapy and goes whenever he can, even if nothing is troubling him).

The profile of Evans also included several quotes from Downey Jr., who reflected on a decade of working together. “I’ve been in hundreds of scenes with this guy,” he said. “Nobody laughs more than him. Sometimes he makes me self-conscious, like, ‘Should I be more fun?’ There’s a little bit of, like, just trying to shake out the anxiety. And I’ve also seen him, over the last 10 years, go from being someone who had laughably real social anxiety to someone who has grown more and more comfortable in their own skin.”

Downey Jr. also spoke on what he feels makes Evans such an ideal actor for the role of Captain America. “At the root of it, he has true humility,” he said. "I think it's the reason he was able to kind of come to the front and be our team leader in the Avengers. I think a lot of his theater experience helped, too. Because it was like, 'OK, I'm going to dress up, I'm going to go out, and I'm going to tell the truth.' It's very kind of old-school Spencer Tracy. Although I guarantee you Spencer Tracy never would've put on that getup.”

And the way Downey Jr. tells it, Evans’ commitment to his performance not only helped to ground the fantastical stories they were telling, but also enabled everything that came afterwards in the MCU.

"[Evans'] suspension of his own disbelief, regardless of whatever doubts he had, is the reason all these other worlds are able to be built," he said. "Starting with Avengers, and then Guardians, and Black Panther. People love to say—and I'll eat it up—that I'm kind of the progenitor of this whole universe. But if you want to talk about it in terms of team building, and you want to talk about it as the most successful creative relay race in the history of cinema, he was the critical leg.”

It certainly sounds like Evans and Downey Jr. share just as deep a friendship as their on-screen counterparts, albeit one less plagued by complicated backstory. All of which is to say, we’ll miss them both when, in all likelihood, they bow out of the MCU following Avengers: Endgame.
