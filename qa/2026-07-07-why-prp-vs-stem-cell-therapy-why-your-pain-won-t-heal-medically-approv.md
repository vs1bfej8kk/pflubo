Why Your Pain Won't Heal & Medically Approved Next Steps

Chronic joint, tendon or muscle pain can feel like a roadblock to living your best life. You've tried rest, physical therapy and medications, yet discomfort lingers. Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) and stem cell therapy have emerged as two popular "regenerative" options—but which is right for you? Here's an evidence-based look at PRP vs stem cell therapy, why pain sometimes refuses to heal, and the medically approved next steps you can take.

Why Your Pain Sometimes Doesn't Heal

Even with proper rest and standard treatments, lingering pain can stem from:

Degenerative changes: Wear-and-tear (like osteoarthritis) gradually erodes joint cartilage, creating chronic discomfort.

Poor blood flow: Tendons and ligaments have limited circulation, so tiny injuries can stick around.

Inflammation imbalance: Ongoing low-level inflammation prevents repair and prolongs sensitivity.

Scar tissue formation: Repeated minor injuries cause fibrous tissue that restricts normal movement and perpetuates pain.

Underlying health factors: Hormonal imbalances, diabetes or smoking can slow natural healing processes.

Understanding why your body struggles to mend sets realistic expectations for any treatment—whether PRP, stem cells or more traditional therapies.

What Are PRP and Stem Cell Therapy?

Before comparing, let's define each:

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP)

A small sample of your blood is spun in a centrifuge to concentrate platelets, growth factors and proteins that support tissue repair.

Injected into the injured area, PRP is thought to jumpstart a healing cascade by releasing bioactive molecules.

Stem Cell Therapy

Uses stem cells (most often sourced from your own bone marrow or adipose/fat tissue) that have the potential to develop into different tissue types.

Injected or guided into the damaged site, these cells may signal native tissue to regenerate or directly integrate into new tissue.

Both aim to use your body's own regenerative capabilities—but they work in distinct ways.

What the Research Says

A 2021 meta-analysis in the American Journal of Sports Medicine found PRP reduced pain and improved function in knee osteoarthritis better than placebo.

Early clinical trials on mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) suggest potential cartilage thickness improvement, but larger randomized trials are still underway.

Both therapies appear safe when performed by experienced, credentialed physicians—but long-term data is still maturing.

[PRP vs](https://benpepsi.com/)

In short, PRP has a stronger track record in specific tendon and joint conditions today, while stem cell therapy remains an exciting frontier with ongoing research.

Who Might Benefit from Each?

PRP may be a reasonable first step if you have:

Mild to moderate osteoarthritis of the knee or shoulder

Tendon injuries like tennis elbow, Achilles tendinopathy or rotator cuff tendinosis

Labral tears in the hip or shoulder without major structural instability

Stem cell therapy could be considered when:

Cartilage loss is more advanced and you've exhausted PRP or other conservative treatments

You've had previous joint surgery or higher-grade tendon tears

You understand and accept the current experimental nature and cost

Always align treatment choice with your injury severity, recovery goals and budget.

When to Seek Immediate Medical Attention

If you experience any of the following, speak to a doctor or visit an emergency department without delay:

Severe swelling, redness or fever after injection

Sudden loss of joint function or inability to bear weight

Signs of deep infection (increasing pain, warmth, red streaks)

Chest pain, shortness of breath or other alarming symptoms
