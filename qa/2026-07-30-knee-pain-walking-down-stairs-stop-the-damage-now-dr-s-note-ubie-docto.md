Why does knee pain occur when walking down stairs?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Knee pain when walking down stairs is most often caused by increased pressure on the joint—especially behind the kneecap—during descent. Going downstairs can load the knees with up to 3.5 times your body weight, which can trigger pain if cartilage is worn, tendons are irritated, or the joint is damaged.

Explanation

When you go down stairs, your knee bends more and works harder to control your movement. This action increases the pressure on the patellofemoral joint—the part where your kneecap meets your thigh bone.

In people with knee osteoarthritis, the cartilage may be worn thin, which means the smooth cushion that helps the bones glide over each other is damaged. This loss of cushioning leads to more friction and pain.

Additionally, conditions like chondromalacia patella occur when the cartilage on the underside of the kneecap softens or wears away, making movement even more painful during stair descent. The extra force during each step forces the joint to absorb more impact and can exacerbate symptoms.

If you're experiencing persistent knee pain during daily activities like walking down stairs, you can check your symptoms with a free AI-powered tool to better understand what might be causing your discomfort. Studies on the biomechanics of stair descent show that increased knee flexion and joint loading contribute largely to the discomfort. Understanding these factors can help guide exercises and treatments to ease the pain and improve joint motion.
