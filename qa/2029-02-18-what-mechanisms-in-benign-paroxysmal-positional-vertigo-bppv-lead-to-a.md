Internal Medicine

Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) causes a sudden spinning sensation when tiny calcium crystals (otoconia) dislodge from their normal location in the inner ear and drift into the semicircular canals. These misplaced crystals send confusing signals to the brain about head position and movement, triggering brief but intense vertigo—especially when rolling over in bed, looking up, or bending down.

Because dizziness and vertigo can stem from many different causes—from BPPV to migraines, low blood pressure, or inner ear infections—it's important to identify what's actually driving your symptoms before deciding on next steps. Explanation

Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) is a condition where small crystals, called otoliths, become dislodged in the inner ear. These crystals normally help with balance by telling the brain how the head is moving. However, if they move into the wrong part of the ear, they give the brain mixed signals.

How It Works:

Normal Function: In a healthy ear, otoliths stay in one area and help sense gravity and movement.

[lead to](https://appetitecode.jcesqc8.com/)

Dislodgement: In BPPV, these crystals move into the semicircular canals, which are like tubes in your ear that help with balance.

Mixed Signals: When you move your head, these loose crystals make the fluid in the canals move in unusual ways, sending wrong messages to the brain.

Spinning Sensation: The brain gets confused by these mixed signals, leading to dizziness or a spinning feeling, especially when changing positions.
