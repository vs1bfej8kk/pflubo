3. Past Medical and Surgical History

CRS often coexists with other conditions. Listing them helps your doctor understand potential risk factors.

Previous sinus or nasal surgeries (e.g., septoplasty, functional endoscopic sinus surgery)

History of asthma, allergic rhinitis, eczema

Autoimmune or inflammatory conditions (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis, vasculitis)

Frequent upper respiratory infections or pneumonia

Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) or acid reflux

Immune system disorders or treatments (e.g., chemotherapy, immunoglobulin deficiency)

Any bleeding disorders or clotting problems

6. Family History

Genetic predisposition can increase your risk of chronic sinus disease or related conditions.

Immune disorders

7. Lifestyle and Social History

Your daily habits and environment can influence CRS severity and treatment choices.

Tobacco use (current or past), including vaping

Alcohol consumption

Diet (spicy foods, high-sugar intake)

Exercise habits

Sleep quality and position (e.g., elevated head, use of extra pillows)

Stress levels and coping strategies

8. Physical Exam and Objective Findings

Provide any recent examination or diagnostic test results you have on hand.

Nasal endoscopy notes (polyps, mucosal swelling, discharge)

CT scan of sinuses (date and findings)

MRI if performed (rare in CRS)

Lab tests (e.g., complete blood count, IgE levels, allergy panels)

Peak flow or spirometry if asthma is present
