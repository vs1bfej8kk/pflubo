Internal Medicine

Apple cider vinegar (ACV) may support digestion in two key ways: it can slow gastric emptying, which helps regulate blood sugar and reduce bloating after meals, and it may positively influence gut bacteria due to its acetic acid content and trace prebiotic compounds. However, current evidence is limited, and more clinical research is needed to confirm these digestive benefits.

Apple cider vinegar (ACV) has been studied for its potential effects on digestion. One study suggests that ACV might help slow down how quickly the stomach empties, which can aid in digestion. This means that food stays in the stomach longer, allowing for better breakdown and absorption of nutrients.

Another study explored how vinegar can support the gut microbiota, which are the tiny organisms living in your intestines. These changes in gut bacteria might also support better digestion.
