# How to Naturally Increase Testosterone | Gameday Men’s Health

Reviewed by: Dr. Hal and Gameday's Medical Advisory Board

Testosterone levels naturally decline as men age. This process often begins in the early thirties and continues gradually over time, with levels dropping an estimated 1% per year after age 30. (1)

For some men, this decline is subtle and barely noticeable. For others, it can lead to significant changes in energy, body composition, mood, and sexual health that affect day-to-day life.

Lifestyle factors and habits play a significant role in hormone health. Sleep, exercise, nutrition, and stress levels all influence how much testosterone the body produces and how efficiently it is used. (2) While these methods cannot correct a true hormone deficiency on their own, they can support overall healthier testosterone levels which often leads to men feeling more energized and more like themselves.

Testosterone is the primary male sex hormone, but its role extends far beyond reproduction. It also supports multiple systems throughout the body and contributes to both physical and mental health.

Testosterone helps regulate muscle and bone health by supporting lean muscle mass and maintaining bone density. Adequate levels make it easier to build and preserve strength while reducing the risk of bone loss as men age.

Energy levels are also closely tied to testosterone. Men with healthy hormone levels often report better stamina, faster recovery from exercise, and less persistent fatigue. When levels decline, routine daily tasks can often feel more draining. (3)

Mood and cognition are also influenced by testosterone levels. Testosterone can play a role in motivation, mental clarity, and emotional balance. Low levels have been linked to irritability, mood swings, and difficulty concentrating. (4)

When it comes to sexual health, testosterone plays a critical role in supporting libido, erectile function, and sperm production. A lack of sexual desire or erectile dysfunction is often one of the first signs men notice when they are dealing with low testosterone levels.

Can Testosterone Be Increased Naturally?

In some cases, natural approaches can lead to an increase in testosterone. However, it’s important to set realistic expectations. While changing lifestyle habits can help optimize testosterone production for men whose levels are borderline, they will be less effective for men dealing with clinically low levels caused by age or medical conditions.

For men whose testosterone levels need a small boost, improving sleep, reducing stress, and changing unhealthy habits (such as diet, smoking, or high alcohol use) can make a major difference. (5)

Men who benefit most from natural methods are often those who are sedentary, chronically stressed, under-slept, or following restrictive diets. Improving these areas can remove barriers that prevent the body from producing testosterone efficiently.

When it comes to improving testosterone levels, baseline levels are critical. Men with normal or slightly below average levels of testosterone may feel substantially better for longer with proper lifestyle changes. However, those with a true testosterone deficiency may see modest improvements but often still require medical evaluation and treatment to fully address symptoms.

Exercise and Physical Activity

Physical activity is one of the most powerful natural tools for supporting testosterone. However type, frequency, and intensity of the exercises all matter in terms of how much it will affect testosterone levels.

Resistance training is particularly effective. Lifting weights and engaging large muscle groups stimulates short-term increases in testosterone and supports long-term muscle maintenance. Consistency is more important than extreme intensity, especially as men get older. (6)

High-intensity exercise, such as interval training, can also support hormone health. Short bursts of intense effort followed by recovery periods challenge the body in a way that promotes anabolic hormone release.

Alternatively, excessive endurance training can have the opposite effect. Long-duration cardio performed too frequently without adequate recovery can increase cortisol and suppress testosterone production. When it comes to exercise and testosterone, balance is key. Combining strength training with moderate cardiovascular exercise often provides the best results.

Nutrition and Testosterone Support

What men eat directly affects hormone production, including testosterone. Testosterone is synthesized from cholesterol, and the body requires adequate energy and nutrients to maintain healthy levels.

Balanced macronutrients are essential. Diets that are too low in calories or carbohydrates can signal the body to conserve energy, reducing testosterone output. Protein supports muscle maintenance, carbohydrates support training performance, and fats are critical for hormone synthesis. (7)

Healthy fats are also important. Unsaturated fats from sources like olive oil, nuts, seeds, and fatty fish support overall metabolic health and hormone production. Extremely low-fat diets can negatively affect testosterone over time. (7)

Micronutrients such as zinc, magnesium, and vitamin D play supporting roles in testosterone regulation. These nutrients are best obtained through a varied diet that includes whole foods rather than relying on supplement hype or megadoses.

Avoiding extreme dieting is important. Chronic caloric restriction and rapid weight loss can significantly lower testosterone levels, even in otherwise healthy men. (8)

Sleep and Testosterone Production

Sleep is one of the most overlooked factors when it comes to hormonal health. Testosterone production occurs largely during sleep, particularly during deep sleep cycles.

When sleep is consistently shortened or disrupted, testosterone levels can drop quickly. Even a few nights of poor sleep can lead to measurable reductions. (9) Over time, chronic sleep deprivation compounds the problem and contributes to fatigue, weight gain, and low mood.

Sleep quality matters as much as quantity. Seven to eight hours of restorative sleep is ideal for most men, but fragmented sleep or untreated sleep disorders, such as sleep apnea, can interfere with hormone production even if total sleep time seems adequate.

Stress Management and Cortisol

Stress and testosterone have an inverse relationship. When stress levels rise, the body produces more cortisol. Elevated cortisol suppresses testosterone production and shifts the body into a state focused on survival rather than repair and growth.

Chronic stress, whether it stems from work, financial challenges, family and relationship issues, or other personal responsibilities, can undermine hormone health. Over time, men routinely dealing with high stress levels may notice lower energy levels, difficulty recovering from workouts, and even a reduction in motivation. (10)

Practical stress reduction techniques can help rebalance hormones. Regular physical activity, adequate sleep, and mental health practices such as counseling and meditation can play a key role in keeping testosterone levels at a healthy level. Even small daily habits can make a difference when practiced consistently.

Habits That Lower Testosterone

Some lifestyle habits are known to suppress testosterone production, especially when they become chronic patterns. While these habits may not affect testosterone production in moderation, consistently indulging in them can lead to a worsening of symptoms over time. These examples include:

Excessive alcohol consumption, which interferes with hormone signaling

Smoking, which affects circulation and endocrine function

Chronic caloric restriction or “yo-yo” dieting

A sedentary lifestyle with minimal physical activity

Reducing or fully eliminating these habits can help remove common obstacles to healthy testosterone levels and support overall wellness. However, these methods won’t have as big of an impact on men who already have chronically low testosterone or have low T caused by a medical condition.

When Natural Methods Aren’t Enough

While natural strategies can be extremely valuable tools for increasing testosterone, their effectiveness can be limited in certain cases. For instance, men with symptoms such as persistent fatigue, low libido, erectile dysfunction, depressed mood, or loss of muscle mass may be experiencing clinical low testosterone that can’t be corrected simply with lifestyle changes.

In these cases, lab testing is essential to determine actual hormone levels and identify whether testosterone deficiency is present. From there, a clinician can suggest more in-depth strategies that are designed to raise testosterone levels from chronically low levels.

Medical evaluation helps distinguish between lifestyle-related suppression and true hormonal imbalance.
