Published on: 7/21/2026

How can you tell the difference between shingles and other skin rashes?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Shingles is a viral skin rash caused by the reactivation of the chickenpox virus (varicella-zoster). It typically appears as a painful, blistering band or stripe on one side of the body or face, often accompanied by burning, tingling, or itching. Other common rashes—such as eczema, hives, or contact dermatitis—usually appear on both sides of the body, vary in shape, and are rarely painful in the same way.

Because shingles can lead to complications like long-term nerve pain if left untreated, identifying it early matters.
