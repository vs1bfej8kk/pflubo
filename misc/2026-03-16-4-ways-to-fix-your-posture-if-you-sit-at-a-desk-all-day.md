# 4 Ways to Fix Your Posture if You Sit at a Desk All Day

[Posture if You Sit](https://thymicshield.dns0663.com/blog/6088869351.html)

Even if you have an active lifestyle and work out regularly, it's likely that your average day involves a lot of sitting. And with many people working from home during the pandemic, with less than ideal home office setups, bad posture is an ever-present risk.

[You Sit](https://calmvector.dns0663.com/)

In the most recent Athlean-X video, Jeff Cavaliere C.S.C.S. explains how sitting for longer than six hours at a time can result in lower cross syndrome, which can cause tightness in the hip flexors and weakness in the glutes, in addition to a whole other range of problems including poor circulation, decreased insulin sensitivity, and and a massive dip in the fat-burning hormone lipoprotein lipase.

[You Sit](https://glycometrix.snaketongueofficial.com/blog/7246791221.html)

In order to avoid these issues and start to correct the postural problems that you may be experiencing due to your work habits, Cavaliere outlines four simple tactics that you can incorporate into your daily routine, starting with a morning hang.

"It's really important that you try to decompress your spine, realizing how much you're going to be sitting on it during the day, first thing in the morning," he says, suggesting hanging from a pullup bar or even just holding your bodyweight up on a countertop so that your spine can hang down.

"You don't have to worry about picking your feet up off the ground," he adds. "As a matter of fact, you want your toes to remain in contact with the ground, to allow the pelvis to drop and allow the spine to decompress. If you do this right, you'll feel the decompressions level by level along the lumbar spine."

[Fix Your Posture](https://peplawllc.com/blog/9066947049.html)

If you spend long stretches of time sitting and working at a computer, then Cavaliere advises setting an alarm for 30 minutes. When the alarm goes off, simply stand up and grab your butt, with the heel of your palm at the top, and push forward for 15 to 20 seconds. "This ensures that we're getting a healthy extension of the lower back, because we're controlling the pelvis and not just pushing in the area of the lumbar spine," he explains.

Then, before sitting down again, repeat the exercise, this time squeezing the glutes and the shoulders together as you do so. Hold for 30 seconds. "This should feel amazing," says Cavaliere.

Set the timer for 30 minutes again, and when it goes off, Cavaliere recommends another stretch: the bridge reach over, which loosens up the hips, back and shoulders, and which he has previously suggested as something to include in your early morning routine. Simply lie on your back with your knees at a 90-degree angle, arch your glutes in a normal bridge motion, and try to touch the ground above your right shoulder with your left arm. Then attempt to do the same with the opposite arm. Repeat this on each side 5 times.

"It doesn't go unnoticed, the benefits you're getting from standing up and simply interrupting that sitting pattern," he says.

Half an hour later, stand up from your desk again, and lower yourself into a deep squat with your back flat against a wall. "We need to have that mobility through our hips," says Cavaliere. "Even though we have flexion of the hips by sitting down, we're really never going anywhere near 90 degrees or more of hip flexion... We want to be able to maintain the ability to get into a deep squat, not to mention that it has additional benefits, like ankle flexibility, that are going to serve you as well."

While still in the squat, keeping your shoulders flat against the wall, reach your hands up above your head to activate the thoracic spine and rotator cuff, and try to touch your hands together. Do this 3 times, and then remain in the squat for 30 seconds.

Throughout the rest of the day, simply repeat these 3 stretches, in sequence, at half-hour intervals. "Rotating through these really simple, easy, short requirements here is going to go a long way towards undoing all that damage that sitting is doing to you," he says.
