Yes, swollen axillary lymph nodes are a common cause of right armpit pain in women. These tender, movement-sensitive lumps typically result from infections, recent vaccinations, or minor injuries. However, seek prompt medical care if the lump is hard, fixed, rapidly growing, larger than 2 cm, or accompanied by fever, night sweats, unexplained weight loss, or breast changes. Doctors may use ultrasound or mammography to evaluate concerning lumps. Other possible causes include hidradenitis suppurativa, cysts, muscle strain, or referred breast pain—each requiring different care approaches, from home remedies to biopsy.

Explanation

Could swollen lymph nodes cause a female's pain under the right armpit?

Swollen (enlarged) lymph nodes in the axilla (armpit) are a common source of discomfort and sometimes pain. In women, understanding when a swollen node is benign versus when it warrants further evaluation can help you take appropriate action without unnecessary anxiety.

Anatomy and Function of Axillary Lymph Nodes

Lymph nodes filter fluid (lymph) and trap bacteria, viruses, and abnormal cells.

The axilla contains 20–40 lymph nodes that drain the arm, chest wall, and breast tissue.

When these nodes react to infection or inflammation, they can enlarge and become tender.

How Swelling Leads to Pain

Capsules around lymph nodes stretch as they fill with immune cells.

Local inflammation releases chemicals that stimulate pain receptors.

Pain often worsens with arm movement or pressure on the armpit.

Common Benign Causes

Insect bites, boils, or cellulitis on the arm or chest wall

Signs: redness, warmth, fever

Colds, flu, throat infections

May cause generalized lymph node enlargement

Vaccines for COVID-19, flu, or tetanus can trigger temporary swelling

Red Flags That Need Prompt Medical AttentionIf you have any of the following, speak to a doctor without delay:

A hard, fixed lump that persists > 2 – 4 weeks

Rapidly growing node

Unexplained weight loss, night sweats, or persistent fevers

Significant breast changes (nipple discharge, skin dimpling)

Widespread lymph node enlargement (neck, groin)

Less Common—but Serious—Causes• Breast cancer metastasis to axillary nodes• Lymphoma (Hodgkin's or non-Hodgkin's)• Tuberculosis or other systemic infections

Other Possible Causes of Right-Side Armpit Pain in Women

Muscular strain (rotator cuff, chest wall)

Referred pain from rib injury or gallbladder disease

Shingles (herpes zoster) before rash appear

Hidradenitis suppurativa (chronic inflammatory skin condition)

What to Expect at Your Doctor's Visit

Physical exam of the armpit, breast, and nearby lymph node regions

Blood tests (complete blood count, inflammatory markers)

Imaging (ultrasound, mammogram, MRI if indicated)

Possible biopsy if malignancy is suspected

Preventive and Supportive Measures

Over-the-counter pain relievers (ibuprofen or acetaminophen)

Rest and elevating the arm when possible

Treat any obvious skin infections promptly
