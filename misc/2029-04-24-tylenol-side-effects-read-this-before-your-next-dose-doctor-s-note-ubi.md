Internal Medicine

Tylenol (acetaminophen) is generally well-tolerated, and most people experience only mild side effects. However, taking too much acetaminophen — even slightly above the recommended dose — can cause serious liver damage, sometimes leading to liver failure. Common mild side effects may include nausea, headache, or rash, while warning signs of overdose include stomach pain, vomiting, loss of appetite, and yellowing of the skin or eyes.

If you're experiencing symptoms after taking Tylenol, or you're unsure whether what you're feeling is normal, it's important to get clarity quickly. Liver-related symptoms can escalate silently, and early awareness makes a meaningful difference in outcomes. Explanation

Tylenol, also known as acetaminophen, is a common medication used to relieve pain and reduce fever. While it is generally safe when taken as directed, it can cause side effects. Most people experience mild side effects, such as nausea or a rash.

However, it's important to be careful about the amount you take. Taking too much Tylenol can lead to serious liver damage, which is a more severe side effect.

Key Points:

Mild Side Effects: These can include stomach upset, nausea, or skin rash. These are not very common and usually not serious.
